A picture gives every Spanish sentence a head start.

When you can see who is speaking, where they are and what is happening, Spanish stops being a collection of words to decode. The story is already giving you clues.

You understand more than the words.

In a comic, language doesn’t arrive alone. A character’s expression, an object they’re holding, the place they’re standing and what happened in the previous panel all help you make sense of what you’re reading.

Pictures give unfamiliar Spanish context. You can often understand the direction of a sentence before you know every word.

Hear the language

The picture is doing some of the teaching.

Traditional reading practice can leave you reaching for a dictionary every few words. Comics add another source of information: the scene itself.

You begin by making a reasonable guess from the picture and the story. When you need help, you check the language. Over time, more of those guesses become understanding.

You don’t need to understand every word to understand what’s happening. And wanting to understand what’s happening gives you a reason to learn the words you don’t know.

In Comigo, the story becomes the lesson.

Read→ Listen→ Explore→ Speak→ Practise

Read

Follow the story in Spanish, one bubble at a time.

Listen

Hear the dialogue spoken with tone and character.

Explore

Tap unfamiliar words and reveal explanations or translations when you need them.

Speak

Say the dialogue yourself and practise producing the Spanish, not just recognising it.

Practise

After the episode, work with the vocabulary and sentences you have just encountered in the story.
